An authorization lock reserves part of one wallet asset until an expiry slot. It
works like a card authorization hold: a role places a hold on funds, every other
role is kept off them, and the holder later spends them. The holder's spending
is the capture.

Locks are stored on the Swig config account and are enforced on every `SignV2`
execution, on both the `All` path and the limited path.

This feature is pending the authorization-lock program release. The Protocol SDK
TypeScript implementation lives in [swig-protocol-sdk](https://github.com/anagrambuild/swig-protocol-sdk/tree/main/solana/typescript).
The Rust port and hosted Developer SDK support are under review; the published
Rust protocol crates at `3.0.0` do not expose the lock APIs below.

## What a lock covers

Each lock records:

| Field | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `lock_id` | sequential id, unique within the wallet and never reused |
| `role_id` | the role that created the lock: the holder |
| `asset_account` | the wallet address (native SOL) or one token account owned by it |
| `mint` | the token mint, or all zeros for native SOL |
| `amount` | the amount still reserved, in lamports or token base units. It falls as the holder spends |
| `expiry_slot` | the slot at which the lock stops binding |

A lock is active while `amount > 0` and `current_slot < expiry_slot`. Expired
and fully captured locks (`amount == 0`) no longer bind anything, but they stay
stored until someone prunes them. A wallet can store at most 32 locks, inactive
ones included.

## How locks are enforced

### Other roles are held off

While a lock is active, every role other than its holder can spend only the
account's balance minus the active locks that role does not hold. This
includes `All`, `AllButManageAuthority`, and the owner. After each `SignV2`,
each writable locked account must end at or above
`min(balance_before, reserved_by_others)`, where `reserved_by_others` is the sum
of the active locks on that account held by roles other than the signer.

The account can always receive funds. Native SOL balances only count lamports
above the wallet address's rent-exempt minimum.

### The holder spends, and spending captures

The holder's own locks don't restrict it. When the holder signs a `SignV2`, the
program measures each locked account's net outflow (`before - after`, never
negative) and takes it off the holder's locks on that account. The oldest lock
(lowest `lock_id`) goes first, and no lock goes below 0. The new amounts are
written back to the config account in place.

Outflow beyond the holder's locks comes from free balance. The holder still
can't spend into another role's lock, because the floor above applies to it
too. Inflows never raise a lock.

A lock captured to 0 stays stored with `amount == 0`. It is inactive, and it
stays stored until it is pruned with `RemoveAuthorizationLockV1` or removed
along with its holder's role.

Locks don't change what `SignV2` needs or touches:

- it needs no extra accounts because of locks. A transaction's accounts are the
  same with or without them;
- it never resizes the config account or moves lamports for locks. A capture
  only rewrites the lock's stored amount in place.

### Worked example

The wallet holds 100 SOL above its rent-exempt minimum. Bob's role holds
`ManageAuthorizationLocks` and a 100 SOL limit.

| Step | Balance | Bob's lock | Other roles can spend |
|------|---------|------------|------------------------|
| Bob locks 60 | 100 | 60 | 40 |
| Bob spends 25. The lock is captured by 25 | 75 | 35 | 40 |
| The owner (`All`) spends 40 | 35 | 35 | 0 |
| The owner tries to spend 1 more: `AuthorizationLockViolated` | 35 | 35 | 0 |
| Someone deposits 20 | 55 | 35 | 20 |
| Bob spends 45. 35 is captured, 10 comes from free balance | 10 | 0 (fully captured) | 10 |

Bob's limit is charged on each spend: 25, then 45, leaving 30. After the last
step his lock is inactive. It no longer blocks anything, and any admin role can
prune it.

With several holders, each is held off the others' locks. If the balance is 100,
Bob holds 50 and Carol holds 30, then Bob can bring the balance down to 30,
Carol to 50, and every other role to 80.

### Other protections

These apply while any lock on the account is active, whoever signs, the holder
included:

- A locked token account must keep the wallet as its authority, with no
  delegate and no foreign close authority. A mint's freeze authority can still
  freeze or thaw the account, and a frozen account stays guarded.
- The wallet cannot be closed with `CloseSwigV1`, and a locked token account
  cannot be closed with `CloseTokenAccountV1`.

Token locks need an initialized token account. Token-2022 mints with a
permanent delegate are rejected, because a permanent delegate could move the
tokens without the wallet's signature.

## Who can create a lock

`AddAuthorizationLockV1` creates a lock. Each lock carries its own amount and
expiry. The acting role can use any authority type, including `ProgramExec` and
`ProgramExecSession`, and must hold `ManageAuthorizationLocks
{ max_duration_slots }`. `All` alone is not enough.

- **Duration (every role):** `expiry_slot - current_slot` must be at most the
  role's `max_duration_slots`.
- **Amount, for roles that can spend everything:** a role that also holds
  `All` or `AllButManageAuthority` can lock up to the account's unreserved
  balance.
- **Amount, for every other role:** the role can lock any SOL or token asset,
  but its active locks on that asset, including the new one, must fit within
  what its own spending limits for the asset still allow. For example, a role
  with `ManageAuthorizationLocks` and limits on SOL, USDC and USDT can lock each
  of those up to its limit, and cannot lock an asset it has no limit for.
- **Every role:** active locks on an account, from all holders, can't exceed its
  balance (`AuthorizationLockExceedsBalance`).

A role cannot set or change its own `max_duration_slots`. Another role with
`ManageAuthority` (or `All`) grants or changes it, and a role may always drop
the permission from itself. The root role is the exception: no other role can
manage it, so it sets its own bound.

The payer funds the config account's rent growth for each new lock.

## Limits are charged on spend

Locking checks the holder's limits but charges nothing. The holder's normal
SOL, token, recurring and destination limits are charged when it spends through
`SignV2`, just as without a lock.

For a limited holder, `SignV2` runs in this order: execute, charge limits,
capture locks, then check that the holder's remaining active locks on each mint
still fit what its limits allow. If they don't, the transaction fails with
`AuthorizationLockExceedsSpendLimit`, so a role can't keep a hold it could never
spend. Roles with `All` or `AllButManageAuthority` have no limits to check.

For example, Bob has a 1,000 token limit and a 600 lock on token account A:

- spending 400 from another token account B leaves a 600 limit, which still
  covers the 600 lock;
- spending 401 from B fails, because 599 would not cover 600;
- spending from A charges the limit and captures the lock by the same amount,
  so the two fall together.

Capacity is what `SignV2` would allow at the current slot. One-shot limits use
their remaining amount. Recurring limits use what's left in the current window,
or the full allowance once the window would reset. With both a general and a
destination limit, the smaller applies.

## Releasing and pruning

`RemoveAuthorizationLockV1` deletes a lock. The holder doesn't need to remove a
lock to spend it.

- **Release:** the holder can remove its own lock at any time. This voids
  whatever is still reserved.
- **Prune:** after a lock expires or is fully captured, any role holding `All`,
  `AllButManageAuthority`, `ManageAuthority`, or `ManageAuthorizationLocks`
  can remove it to reclaim space. The holder can too.
- No other role can remove an active lock. This includes the root `All` role.
- **With the role:** removing a holder's role with `RemoveAuthorityV1` also
  prunes its inactive locks. See
  [Removing a lock holder](#removing-a-lock-holder).

Rent freed by removal goes to the configured
[rent claimer](/developer-sdk/rent-claimers) if one is set, otherwise to the
payer.

## Instruction accounts

`AddAuthorizationLockV1` (discriminator `25`):

| Index | Account | Notes |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 0 | Swig config | writable |
| 1 | Swig wallet address | read-only; must be the wallet's canonical PDA |
| 2 | Payer | writable, signer; funds the config's rent growth |
| 3 | System program | |
| 4 | Asset account | read-only; the wallet address for SOL, or a token account it owns |
| 5 | Mint | read-only; token locks only, required for Token-2022 |

`RemoveAuthorizationLockV1` (discriminator `26`):

| Index | Account | Notes |
|-------|---------|-------|
| 0 | Swig config | writable |
| 1 | Swig wallet address | read-only; must be the wallet's canonical PDA, otherwise `InvalidSeedSwigAccount` |
| 2 | Payer | writable, signer; receives freed rent when no rent claimer is set |
| 3 | System program | |
| 4 | Rent claimer | writable; only when the wallet has one configured |

Authority accounts follow, as for other instructions: the Ed25519 or session
key signer, or the instructions sysvar for Secp256r1 and `ProgramExec` roles.

The Swig config and wallet address come first so `ProgramExec` roles can
authorize locks. The preceding instruction that a `ProgramExec` role
authenticates against must pass the Swig config and the wallet address, in that
order, as its first two accounts. A `ProgramExecSession` role opens a session
through its program as usual, then signs lock instructions with the session
key.

## Effect on role management

A lock is only useful if its holder stays able to spend it. While a role holds
any active lock, even if its other locks are inactive:

- it cannot be removed with `RemoveAuthorityV1`, not even by itself. This fails
  with `AuthorizationLockActive`
- another role cannot replace its authority with `ReplaceAuthorityV1`. Its locks
  don't block it from rotating its own authority
- another role cannot update its actions with `UpdateAuthorityV1`, except for a
  `RemoveActionsByType` that removes only `ManageAuthorizationLocks`

Removing `ManageAuthorizationLocks` stops the role from creating new locks.
Existing locks still run to expiry, and the holder can still spend or release
them. A delegate therefore cannot make itself irrevocable by continually
renewing locks.

Once all of a role's locks are inactive (expired or fully captured), these
restrictions lift. `UpdateAuthorityV1` and `ReplaceAuthorityV1` then work as
usual and leave the role's lock records in place.

### Removing a lock holder

A role whose locks are all inactive can be removed with `RemoveAuthorityV1`.
The usual permission rules apply. A role with `All` or `ManageAuthority` can
remove it, and so can the role itself. `AllButManageAuthority` can't remove
other roles.

The same instruction prunes the role's lock records:

- its inactive locks are deleted, and their rent is refunded with the role's,
  to the rent claimer if one is set, otherwise to the payer
- other roles' locks and the wallet's lock-id counter are kept
- role ids and lock ids are never reused, so a role added later inherits
  nothing from the removed one

Because every lock is bounded by its creator's `max_duration_slots`, the
longest a role can be pinned is the duration someone else granted it.

<Note>
An existing wallet's root role cannot lock until it grants itself
`ManageAuthorizationLocks`, for example with `UpdateAuthorityV1` `AddActions`
on role 0.
</Note>

## Durations are measured in slots

`max_duration_slots` and `expiry_slot` count slots, not seconds. Slot time is
dropping toward 200ms, so a duration sized for 400ms slots ends sooner in
wall-clock time than intended. See
[time-based limits](/protocol/concepts-and-permissions#time-based-limits-are-measured-in-slots).
